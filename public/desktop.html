Controls & mission briefing

SBCC: Two playable floors adapted from the supplied Scharnberg floor plans: auditorium and balcony, west offices, central classrooms, café 144, and cybersecurity 229/228. The same controls, difficulties, and game modes apply. Dr. White is the only entity here. His one-use double-speed boost starts after 28–52 seconds and lasts ten seconds. SBCC rooms use their own solid furniture set: executive desks, swivel chairs, pedestal meeting tables, upholstered sofas, filing cabinets, presentation displays, theater seats and dual-screen security consoles. Every room keeps its own fixed placement profile. The café has fixed physical counters, a waist-high swing gate, drinks, tables, chairs and benches. Small offices are merged into clearly enclosed rooms; halls and stair landings are not collectible rooms. The third ENS floor is not present here.

Puzzle Mode: Find twelve paper fragments: six parts of a highlighted-pattern sheet and six parts of its digit legend. Open Inventory (I on desktop, Inventory button on mobile) to see held team fragments assembled automatically. Four gold patterns labeled STEP 1–4 specify the code order. The nine pattern-to-digit assignments and highlighted order change every round. Getting captured drops all carried fragments and removes them from the shared sheets until recovered. Use the front-door keypad with F / USE; click digits or type 1–9 on desktop, tap buttons on mobile. The fourth digit submits automatically. A wrong code makes every active entity track the triggering student for ten seconds; lockers and vents still protect hidden students. The correct code opens the doors for everyone to walk out. The twelve fragments stay fixed across difficulties; entities scale as usual.

Choose a playable map above, then Play solo or Join multiplayer. Coming soon tiles are placeholders for future maps. Choose Key Mode or Puzzle Mode above the difficulty, then choose Easy, Normal, Hard, or Extreme. Key Mode uses carried keys and door locks; harder settings add keys. Puzzle Mode uses the same ENS building with twelve paper fragments and a randomized keypad code; its fragment count stays fixed while entity difficulty changes. Normal preserves the original entity settings. More players strengthen the entities in both modes. In multiplayer, the host chooses the map, game mode, and difficulty; these settings stay locked during a round.

WASD move · ← → turn (or drag the view / click for mouse look) · F interact / collect / rescue / locker / elevator · Space jump · Shift + F crawl through a vent · M map · Walk onto stairs to climb or descend · F activate the small elevator floor button under the crosshair · Esc menu.

Keys belong to the student who picks them up. Only that carrier can use a key on the front-door locks. Press F away from nearby objects to drop one carried key exactly where you are standing; another student can collect it with F. Being caught by either enemy drops every carried key at the capture point before you are moved, even if a jail card saves you. A disconnect also leaves carried keys at that student’s last known position. You cannot drop keys while hiding in a locker or traveling through a vent. Loose keys inside the elevator travel with the car.

The large yellow counter shows TEAM-carried keys / locks remaining. All players see the same total; keys still belong to their carriers, and your own key count appears in the team list. For example, collecting two keys and using one changes your counter from 2 / 4 to 1 / 3. Gold bars show locks remaining, and the objective separately tracks keys still to find. There is still one matching lock for every key. After all locks are removed, press F to open the actual front doors, then walk through them to escape. Every student must walk outside to win. Once the exit doors open, all active entities gain permanent double speed and building-wide tracking until the round ends. Lockers and vents still protect hidden students. A free student can use F at room 205 to release everyone inside. The team loses only when every student is jailed. Escaped students can press F / USF to re-enter if anyone is jailed. A rescued student gets five seconds of immunity; the rescuer gets two seconds. When each timer ends, their current location is revealed for a ten-second investigation. Jailed students may walk and turn inside their barred, empty cell but cannot interact or leave until rescued. One guy and one girl are hugging in a random room each round. Press F to meet the Cedar couple. The first finder receives one jail card, automatically consumed on their next capture. The couple grants only one card per round and disappears immediately after the first encounter, including its map marker.

Hold Shift to crouch (toggle CROUCH on mobile): your viewpoint and character lower, and movement is 25% slower. Unaware entities must come within 1.25 tiles with a clear line of sight to spot you. Entities already pursuing you keep their usual sight and memory; noise reveals and the exit finale still work. Crouching lasts at most 15 seconds. Sessions under 10 seconds require twice that duration standing; sessions of 10–15 seconds require an equal-duration recovery. Stairs, jumping, lockers, vents and capture end crouching. Shift + F still enters vents, including during crouch recovery.

Walk onto a staircase and use your movement controls to climb or descend. ENS floor 2 has separate up and down flights; SBCC has one connecting flight between its two floors. Side rails keep you on the steps. Stairs are quiet; no E key or floor menu is needed. On Normal solo, enemies spot you only within 6.5 tiles with a clear line of sight, pursue your last sighting for up to 3.5 seconds, search for four seconds, then wander. Easy shortens sight and search; Hard and Extreme increase them. Each additional connected student adds 0.8% enemy speed and 2.5% sight range, memory and search time, with gently faster route updates. Ordinary multiplayer hunting uses sight and memory; building-wide relentless tracking begins only when the exit opens. ENS entities choose separate routes after seeing each other for three seconds. Captured and escaped students still count toward team size. Use F at the elevator entrance to call the car and open the doors. Walk inside, turn toward the eye-level buttons on the side wall, aim the crosshair at 1, 2, or 3, and press F. An outside call’s first opening chime schedules an investigation after 8 seconds; enemies already chasing you remain dangerous. Choosing a different floor immediately blocks the doorway and closes the doors, starts a three-second trip, and redirects enemies from their current positions to the destination. The doors open automatically on arrival, with a separate 15-second investigation delay for that destination opening. All students inside share the ride. After the entities reach the elevator and finish their three-second inspection, they close the doors in place. Closing waits until students and entities have cleared the car and doorway. Use F to call or open it again; the normal opening alert delays still apply. Difficulty adjusts when students join or leave; keys and map size stay locked until the next round. Lockers appear against walls in a few random rooms at the start of each round, never in rooms with vents. They have no map markers; find them by exploring the rooms. They stay in place for the whole round. Press F near a locker to hide, and E again to leave. One student fits in each locker. Enemies cannot see or catch a hidden student. Hiding forces the floor map to remain open, prevents movement, and lasts at most 15 seconds; then you automatically exit at that same locker. Vents are placed in random rooms at the start of each round and stay there until the next round. Hold Shift and press F near a vent to enter a four-second crawl through a metal duct. Only the student who enters sees the duct and travels; all other students keep playing normally. During the last part of the four-second crawl, the exit hatch swings open and you crawl through it into the destination room, then rise to standing. You emerge at a randomly chosen vent in a different room, possibly on another floor. Movement and interactions are unavailable while crawling; enemies cannot see or catch you in the duct. The elevator car serves every playable floor of the selected building; another student may call it from a landing when it is idle. Once per round, Ayers uses an energy power-up and moves at double speed for exactly ten seconds. The lobby expands at 2, 5, 8, 11 and 14 players, adding more rooms, stairs and vents. Normal exit keys = 4 + half the extra players, rounded up. Easy starts at 3 keys, Hard at 6, and Extreme at 8, with that same team-size increase. Every key still matches one door lock. The map and key goal lock when the host starts. Late joins use that layout until the next round. Enemy portraits appear on the current floor’s maps only while standing in a tile any teammate has explored. They disappear again when they enter unexplored territory; the sidebar never reports enemy floors. Teammates appear as larger blue direction arrows; your arrow is yellow and jailed teammates are orange. Stairs, vents, elevator landings, and other landmarks appear on either map only after any teammate discovers their locations. Keys and lockers never appear on either map. This is a fictional campus game; maps are adapted from your photos for gameplay, not an exact building survey.

Separate games can run at the same time: choose a unique, case-sensitive room name for each group. Use Copy room link to invite friends. Everyone opens that link, chooses Join multiplayer, and the first student hosts. The host chooses the map, mode and difficulty, waits for the group, then chooses Start game in the visible multiplayer lobby or Start multiplayer game in the room menu. After a round, the host returns everyone to the lobby, changes map, mode or difficulty, and starts again with the same connected group. In the lobby, the host may edit the room name and choose Rename room for everyone; Copy room link then uses the new name. Each room supports up to 16 students. Solo play works directly from the website. Phones use touch controls; computers use keyboard and mouse. Keep the host tab active. After an update, refresh before starting a new round. Deploying a new server version can interrupt current multiplayer games.

Capture soundtrack: an original synthesized arrangement of the complete “The Doxology” melody, with gentle pipe color, strings and restrained drums (Thomas Ken text / traditional Old Hundredth tune), not a recording or a sample of the meme audio. Tune and attribution. Quiet original ambient music plays during exploration and ducks beneath the capture hymn. Background, capture, chase, footsteps, interaction effects and elevator music have separate volume sliders; Sound mutes everything. Each new chase randomly selects one of two original action scores: Night Signal (188 BPM, distorted bass and alarm synths) or Redline (204 BPM, racing synths and percussion). The choice stays fixed throughout that chase, including mute or menu pauses. Chase music begins three tiles beyond normal enemy sight, with a clear line of sight. When the exit opens, it plays for everyone on every floor until the round ends; capture music takes priority. Locker and vent creaks are cosmetic sounds and never alert entities. Elevator doors slide and ding on opening and closing; elevator music is heard only inside the car.
